People don't just drink coffee at home anymore; they go to cafés [1]. Cafés are not just for grabbing a drink; people go there to study, work, meet friends, or even join small events [2]. Young people, especially students and workers, like going to cafés because the places are quiet, relaxing, and comfortable. Because more people enjoy spending time in cafés, many new independent cafés have opened in the Philippines. Each cafés is different by having unique features, amenities, and service [3]. However, with so many options available, café-goers often face challenges in finding spots that fit their specific needs—such as having fast Wi-Fi, quiet ambiance, pet-friendly policies, or convenient parking [4], [5]. In Davao City, the café scene is unique. Unlike other cities that usually have formed organizations or associations, most local cafés here operate independently. Instead of being with a big organization together with one big group, they often collaborate for events, supporting each other’s launches, or joining community-driven activities. A good example of this is the Davao Coffee Festival, where café owners, baristas, suppliers, and coffee enthusiasts come together to celebrate the city’s vibrant coffee culture [6], [7]. Because of this setup,the researchers plan to work closely with the festival's organizers and administrators. Since they already know many of the established cafés that regularly take part in their events, they can help verify legitimate owners and vouch for cafés that are already recognized by the local coffee community. For smaller or lesser-known cafés that are not yet part of the festival network, additional steps—like submitting business permits, official social media pages, or other proof of ownership—will be required before their café listing is confirmed in the app. This approach makes the platform secure and shows how Davao's coffee culture works, not through one big organization, but through collaboration, shared passion, and community trust. By being in that culture, CoFi ensures that its café listings remain accurate, credible, and trusted by both café-goers and café owners. Meanwhile, apps like Google Maps, TikTok, or Facebook can help people find cafés. However,they usually show only the most popular cafés or those nearest you. They do not tell you if a café is quiet, pet-friendly, or suitable for remote work or studying. Café-related apps, like Roasters and Bean There, allow users to rate cafés and use filters. But these apps do not have detailed features like tags for amenities, community posts, or local event information. These features are important when people are choosing the best café to visit [4], [5]. Additionally,they are not tailored to specific areas like Davao City, limiting their usefulness for local users. On the other hand,the PickUp Coffee mobile app is available in Davao City and has a useful feature, a rewards system. However, this app is made only for the PickUp Coffee chain. It does not support open café discovery or listings for independent local cafés [8]. These existing café-related platforms are limited by scope and functionality, making it difficult for café goers in Davao City to find café venues that truly align with their lifestyle or preferences.Studies in similar areas have shown that systems that learn from what users like or dislike can help people discover new places, a recommendation system that learns from user feedback,  using collaborative filtering, can offer results based on similar user preferences [9]. These systems work well even if the app is just starting and doesn’t have a lot of user data yet [10]. However, most of the existing studies and apps using this technology are based in other countries and focus on international businesses or tourist attractions. No target local café culture or cities in the Philippines—especially places like Davao City—where local preferences and café environments are unique [11].The researchers conducted a survey involving two key respondent groups: café goers and local café owners. The first group—café-goers—participated in an online survey distributed via Google Forms. This group was composed of a diverse group of participants, with the majority (80.6%) aged between 18 and 25, followed by 11.1% aged 25 to 45, 5.6% aged 45 and above, and 2.8% under 18. The second group consisted of six local café owners based in Davao City, who were engaged through face-to-face interviews using the same set of structured questions. These local café owners represent the supply side of the local café business environment and provide insights into the operational challenges and promotional limitations faced by independent cafés in a competitive market. While initial reactions from owners were cautious, their interest grew as the features of the CoFi platform were explained—such as manageable café profiles, amenity-based tagging, event and job listing capabilities, and user review management tools. Findings from the café-goer group revealed that 61.1% experience difficulty finding cafés that align with their preferences. Also, 91.7% expressed a strong desire for recommendations that can fit their preferences, especially those sorted by ambiance, amenities, and service. When asked about specific criteria in choosing a café, ambiance was the most valued factor (58%), followed by reliable Wi-Fi (53%), proximity or accessibility (50%), and pet-friendliness (19%). On the other hand, café owners said they mainly use social media like Facebook and Instagram to promote their cafés. But even though many people use these platforms,they do not always help small, independent cafés get enough visibility. Because of this, it is hard for these cafés to compete and attract new customers. The café owners expressed a strong interest in a more focused and accessible tool—such as CoFi—that could enhance their promotional reach, showcase their cafés unique offerings, and support consistent customer engagement through recommendations, discovery, amenity tagging, and authentic user reviews. The café owners said that a platform like CoFi could help them in reaching new customers through recommendations, user-generated reviews, and targeted promotions. With the current number of eight confirmed local café owner participants, the researchers acknowledge the importance of engaging more café owners in the future to diversify perspectives. In line with this, the researchers have reached out to four more café owners who are in the process of being engaged. This continued outreach supports the recommendation to involve increased participation from the local café sector. In the course of reaching out to café owners,  one confirmed affiliation with the Davao Coffee Circle, a local network of café owners, baristas, suppliers, creatives, and coffee enthusiasts. Although not a formal legal organization, the Davao Coffee Circle functions as an active and credible community within Davao City's coffee industry, they are a significant role in promoting collaboration and mutual support among its members. The Davao Coffee Circle is a good opportunity for CoFi to build trust and connect with the local café community. CoFi and the Davao Coffee Circle can work together in several ways. These include checking café entries sent by users, inviting more café owners to join the platform, and sharing feedback about the app's features and updates. This partnership will strengthen trust in CoFi cafe' listing platform and support long-term engagement from the local café industry. CoFi is envisioned as a community-driven café discovery platform that is not built for any single café or brand. Café owners are expected to hold a designated user role that enables them to manage their respective café pages—such as uploading photos, listing amenities, posting events, and responding to user reviews—while system administration remains with the CoFi core team to ensure fair and secure platform management. These ideas show that there is a need for a platform that helps both café-goers and café owners. CoFi supports this by giving users a better way to discover cafés that match their likes, while also helping café owners grow their business. CoFi addresses this need through a collaborative filtering approach that tailors discovery to individual user preferences, creating a more meaningful and efficient café discovery process.Although café-related apps like Roasters and Bean There provide features such as café search and reviews, they are missing key tools that users expect. These apps do not give recommendations based on what the user likes. They also don't have community lists or options for café owners to update their café profiles or post about events. Because of this, users may miss out on great cafés that fit their needs, and café owners may lose potential loyal customers [5], [11].To address these gaps, the researchers aim to develop CoFi—a mobile-based application that helps users in Davao City discover cafés tailored to their preferences. CoFi uses collaborative filtering, a data-driven approach that arranges cafés in the user’s dynamic feed based on behavior and ratings of similar users [12]. For instance, if two people like cozy spots with outlets, the app can suggest new cafés visited by one but not the other. It also integrates GPS for distance-based searches and lets users filter by amenities such as pet‑friendly spaces, outdoor seating, or study zones. Café owners can manage their menus, amenities, events, and reviews in-app.A collaborative filtering algorithm, which makes recommendations and discovery by comparing a user's activity with others who have similar preferences [5]. This approach works well even without extensive data. To compare users, CoFi applies the Cosine Similarity Index (CSI), which measures the closeness of rating patterns by computing the cosine of the angle between user–café rating vectors. This system uses a similarity score to look at what users like and find other users with similar café choices, even if there is not much data [13]. Then, it uses this to suggest cafés the user might enjoy, even if they haven't rated any yet. This helps the app show cafés that the user will likely find interesting and valuable. The app also uses Firebase to keep user data safe and Google Maps to help with location. This allows the app to show cafés nearby that fit what the user is looking for. Similar approaches using Google Maps for recommendation filtering have been validated in mobile-based recommendation systems [13]. CoFi is more than a café finder—it's a local community partner. Users can search based on their personal needs, like finding a quiet café for studying or a lively one for meeting friends. Café owners also benefit by getting more visibility, connecting directly with customers, and understanding what people are looking for. CoFi unites discovery and local businesses in one platform.To maintain data accuracy while supporting community involvement, the app will have an admin user who checks the content. This is especially important for community-added cafés. These cafés will be checked carefully before they are shown in the app. Users who add cafés must upload a photo of the café's front, provide its location on Google Maps, and add at least one social media link. The system can cross-check this data to verify legitimacy. Entries will remain in a “pending” state until reviewed by an admin. Café owners may also “claim” a listing by submitting verification materials such as a business permit or official social media page.A feature comparison between CoFi and existing café-related platforms like Roasters, Bean There, and PickUp Coffee. The system supported two user roles: café-goers and café-owners. Café-goers could discover cafés through a dynamic explore feed by collaborative filtering, search and filter cafés based on GPS proximity, ambiance, and amenity tags (such as Wi-Fi, power outlets, parking, and pet-friendliness), log café visits, submit reviews and ratings, save cafés into collections, and view café-related events and job postings. Café-owners could manage their café profiles, update amenities and operating hours, post and manage events and job listings, and respond to customer reviews. The recommendation mechanism focused on user-based collaborative filtering using explicit star ratings as the primary input, with visit tags and amenities recorded for contextual refinement. The development and testing of the application were conducted within one academic year, from requirements analysis and UI/UX design to functional validation of the prototype.The study was limited to cafés operating within Davao City and relied on active participation from users to generate sufficient data for recommendation accuracy; as a result, early usage may experience data sparsity that could affect recommendation quality. The collaborative filtering algorithm utilized explicit star ratings as the main parameter, while visit tags and amenities were used as supporting contextual data and were not fully weighted during initial deployment. The accuracy of location-based services depended on the Google Maps API and the availability of device GPS and internet connectivity, which could affect performance in areas with weak signals. Evaluation focused primarily on functional testing and objective validation rather than large-scale usability studies or long-term user behavior analysis. Additionally, the system did not evaluate café quality objectively and relied solely on user-generated data, which may be influenced by subjective preferences.METHODS Figure 1. Conceptual FrameworkInputCoFi collects real-time data from two distinct user roles within its mobile application. Café-goers create their own profiles and choose what they like using tags like &quot;Power Outlets&quot; or &quot;Pet Friendly&quot;, and also set how near the café should be. They can also log their café visits by giving star ratings, writing reviews, and choosing the type of visit, like if it was for studying or hanging out. Users can save cafés to their personal lists, join events, or apply for jobs posted by cafés. Café-Owners manage their café profiles by inputting information, including café name, location, amenities, descriptions, operating hours, social media links, and images. They also post events and job listings directly within the application. All interactions and updates are captured and stored immediately in Cloud Firestore, forming the foundational dataset used by the recommendation engine and UI updates [16]ProcessThe process of the system starts when a user signs up in the CoFi application. During sign-up, the user answers a short survey. In the survey, they choose what kind of visits they like (for example: Study Session, Hangout, or Date) and what amenities they want (like Wi-Fi, outlets, parking, or pet-friendly areas). These choices are saved in the user's profile and are later used as post-filters to refine recommendations according to individual lifestyle needs.After registration, users interact with the system by browsing cafés and leaving explicit star ratings (1–5) in the review page. These ratings are stored in the database and form the input for the collaborative filtering engine. The application constructs a user–café rating matrix where each entry represents the score of user u for café i.The system then applies the Cosine Similarity Index to compare users[17]. If two users give similar ratings to the same cafés, the system assumes they share similar preferences. This similarity is expressed mathematically as:Where:Xp = rating of the first user on the p-th caféYp = rating of the second user on the p-th cafén = number of cafés both users have ratedUsing these similarity scores, the system calculates predictions for cafés that the user has not yet rated through a weighted-sum prediction formula. These predicted scores determine the ranking of cafés in the dynamic explore feed. Finally, the system applies the survey preferences (visit tags and amenities) as filters and ranking boosts, ensuring that the recommendations are both mathematically accurate and contextually relevant.OutputCoFi shows different results depending on the user role. Café-Goers see an up-to-date dynamic café list, an interactive map of nearby spots, their saved collection lists, and a feed of local events and jobs. Café-Owners see lists of reviews and visit logs, counts of event participants, tools to manage their events and jobs, and their shop profile pages. Every visit log, review, saved café, or RSVP updates Firestore and refreshes the dynamic café feed so everyone always sees the latest community activity.2.3.1.4 Collaborative Filtering Technique 2.3.1.4.1 Dataset ConstructionTable 5. The client is a Flutter app that runs on both Android and iOS, hosting all user interfaces (Explore, Community, Collections, Profile, Owner Dashboard), the role-switch controller, and an on-device dynamic café feed engine. Backend infrastructure is provided by Firebase: Authentication secures sign-in via Google, Apple, or email/password and enforces role-based access; Cloud Firestore serves as a NoSQL document store with real-time synchronization for all entities (Users, Cafés, Visits, Reviews, Events, Jobs, RSVPs, Collections, CafeImages, CafeHours); and Cloud Functions run business logic (shop-claim moderation, scheduled “Featured Shops,” and push-notification dispatch). Geo-spatial discovery map rendering is powered by the Google Maps API. All user actions (visit logs, reviews, favorites, RSVPs) write back to Firestore, and real-time listeners immediately update the dynamic café feed to reflect the latest community activity. Figure 5. Behind the scenes, Firebase Authentication secures access, Cloud Firestore stores and synchronises all data, Cloud Functions handle moderation and scheduled “Featured Shops” updates, and the Google Maps API powers all map and location features.2.3.1.9 Sample PrototypesFigure 6. Network connectivity enables communication between the mobile application and Firebase services, as well as access to the Google Maps API for location-based café discovery.Network configuration was accomplished by integrating Firebase configuration files into the Flutter project and enabling the necessary permissions within the application manifest. API keys required for map services were securely configured within the application environment to ensure proper access while preventing unauthorized use. Firestore security rules were implemented to enforce role-based access control, ensuring that users can only access and modify data relevant to their assigned roles. Results And DiscussionThis section presents and discusses the results obtained from the development and testing of the CoFi mobile application. The results are analyzed based on the study’s objectives and focus on the effectiveness of the collaborative filtering mechanism, system functionality, and overall support for café discovery and café-owner engagement in Davao City.The implementation of user-based collaborative filtering successfully generated personalized café recommendations based on explicit user star ratings, with visit tags and amenity preferences used as supporting contextual parameters. Testing showed that users who shared similar rating patterns received overlapping recommendations, confirming that the cosine similarity-based approach functioned as intended. Cafés that were not previously visited by a user were recommended accurately when similar users had rated them positively. These results indicate that collaborative filtering effectively addressed the challenge of café discovery by presenting cafés aligned with individual preferences rather than popularity alone.Weighted parameters allowed star ratings to remain the primary influence on recommendation ranking, while visit tags (e.g., study session, hangout) and amenity preferences (e.g., Wi-Fi, parking) provided contextual refinement. This combination improved the relevance of recommendations, especially for users with goal-based preferences such as studying or working remotely. The findings support existing studies that recommend explicit feedback–based collaborative filtering as an effective approach for early-stage recommender systems with limited data.The integration of GPS-based location services using the Google Maps API enabled users to discover nearby cafés within a defined distance threshold. Testing confirmed that cafés were displayed accurately on the map relative to the user’s location, and proximity filtering functioned consistently across supported devices. Users were able to refine results further using ambiance and amenity filters, allowing more efficient café selection. These results demonstrate that combining recommendation outputs with location-based filtering enhanced usability and decision-making during café discovery.Functional testing verified that café-goers could successfully log visits, submit reviews with star ratings, and organize cafés into collections. Logged visits and reviews were stored in real time and immediately reflected in the dynamic explore feed. Notification testing confirmed that users received alerts for new recommendations, upcoming events, and job postings that matched their preferences. These features contributed to sustained user engagement by encouraging regular interaction with the platform.For café owners, the dashboard allowed successful management of café profiles, amenities, operating hours, and social links. Owners could post, publish, and unpublish events and job listings, as well as respond to user reviews. These capabilities improved visibility and provided café owners with direct feedback from customers, addressing the promotional challenges identified during data gathering.Administrative validation mechanisms ensured that only verified cafés appeared in the platform. Community-added cafés were placed in a pending state until reviewed, helping maintain data accuracy and platform credibility. Compatibility and performance testing showed stable operation across Android and iOS devices, with acceptable response times under normal network conditions. The system consistently met its functional and non-functional requirements, particularly in terms of performance, usability, and security.The results demonstrate that CoFi effectively addressed the primary problem identified in the study: the difficulty of discovering cafés that align with individual user preferences in a localized context. Unlike general mapping or social media platforms, CoFi’s recommendation-driven approach prioritized relevance over popularity. The inclusion of café-owner management tools further distinguished the platform by supporting both demand-side (café-goers) and supply-side (café owners) needs.Overall, the findings confirm that collaborative filtering, when combined with contextual data such as visit tags, amenities, and location, can significantly improve local café discovery. The results also indicate strong potential for CoFi to support community engagement and small business visibility within Davao City’s coffee culture.4. Conclusion and RecommendationThe study concludes with a discussion of the key findings, along with recommendations provided by the research evaluators. These recommendations are intended to support and guide future researchers who may seek to extend or further enhance the study.4.1 ConclusionThis study successfully developed and implemented CoFi, a mobile-based café discovery application designed to help users find cafés in Davao City through a dynamic explore feed. Based on the testing results, the application met both the general and specific objectives stated. The system effectively uses collaborative filtering based on explicit user ratings to arrange cafés, while visit tags are recorded for contextual insights and future improvements.Functional and compatibility testing results confirmed that all major features worked correctly across different devices and network conditions. Algorithm testing verified that the collaborative filtering algorithm correctly ranked cafés based on user similarity, ratings, and preferences, including proper handling of new users. Objectives-based testing further confirmed that features such as GPS-based café discovery, café owner dashboard, community hub, admin validation, and notification system were successfully implemented.Beta testing results from IT experts, a café-goer, and a community representative. IT experts gave high evaluation scores ranging from 90% to 95%, indicating technical performance with minor areas for refinement. The café-goer participant rated the application at 96%, confirming its ease of use and effectiveness in café discovery. The community representative of the Davao Coffee Circle gave a score of 100%, showing full acceptance of the system. Overall, the findings demonstrate that CoFi achieved its objectives.4.1.1 Discussion of Achieved ObjectivesThe first specific objective, implementing the collaborative filtering algorithm, was achieved by collecting user ratings and visit history, then calculating similarity scores between users using cosine similarity. The algorithm was tested using various scenarios, including users with multiple café ratings and new users with no ratings. Through multiple trials, the system successfully predicted high-rated cafés for users and correctly ranked recommendations in the explore feed.The second specific objective, integrating GPS-based café discovery, was achieved by linking the mobile application to Google Maps API. The researchers ensured that café locations were accurately plotted and users could search for nearby cafés using their current location. Testing included searching cafés in different areas of Davao City and verifying that search results were correct and consistent with actual distances.The third specific objective, implementing user discovery features such as filtering, reviews, and collections, was achieved by programming the app to allow users to tag visits, submit reviews with photos, and create collections of favorite cafés. Through iterative testing, the app successfully filtered cafés based on amenities, ambiance, and visit tags, ensuring relevant results for different user preferences.The fourth specific objective, developing the café owner dashboard, was achieved by creating a management interface where café owners could update café profiles, respond to reviews, and post events or job openings. Testing confirmed that owner actions were reflected in the system and that role-based access prevented unauthorized modifications.The fifth specific objective, implementing café validation, was achieved by creating an admin approval workflow that required verification of community-submitted cafés before they appeared on the platform. This process ensured data accuracy and integrity.The sixth specific objective, building the community hub, was achieved by designing features that allowed users to share collections, post discussions, and interact with other users. Testing verified that posts and interactions were visible to the intended audience.The seventh specific objective, enabling notifications, was achieved by integrating Firebase Cloud Messaging to send real-time alerts for updates, new events, or community interactions. Testing ensured that notifications were received on different devices.Finally, the general objective of café discovery application was achieved by combining all these components. Testing, algorithm verification, functional and compatibility checks, and feedback from beta testers confirmed that the application performs as intended, providing an efficient platform for café discovery in Davao City.4.2 RecommendationThe recommendations are aimed at guiding future improvements to the CoFi application, enhancing its functionality, performance, and user experience. It also provides guidance for future researchers who may wish to extend the study, improve the collaborative filtering algorithm, and explore additional features that support café discovery and community engagement.4.2.1 Researchers’ RecommendationThe researchers recommend several improvements and enhancements for future versions of the CoFi application to further enhance both technical performance and user experience. For the collaborative filtering algorithm, future versions could incorporate additional contextual factors such as time of visit or peak hours to make recommendations more accord to user preference. Enhancing data-handling techniques, including optimization of database queries and caching mechanisms, would improve app performance, especially as the number of users and café listings grows.Regarding the user interface, improvements could be made to create a more visually appealing and intuitive experience. Expanding the café profile information, such as including ongoing promotions or special events would provide users with a more understanding of each café. Additionally, future versions could implement a point-based reward system to encourage users’ continued engagement while supporting café promotions and loyalty programs.For the café owner dashboard, the researchers recommend adding advanced analytics features that allow owners to track visit trends, engagement, and user preferences. These insights could help owners make better business decisions, optimize marketing strategies, and improve customer satisfaction. Finally, aspiring researchers and future developers are encouraged to explore more recommendation algorithms, hybrid approaches or context-aware filtering, to further improve the accuracy, efficiency, and relevance of café recommendations.4.2.2 Testers’ RecommendationFeedback from beta testing highlights additional areas for enhancement. IT experts suggested refining existing features to increase system efficiency and responsiveness. They also emphasized the importance of improving the interface design to make it more attractive and intuitive. Feedback from café-goer and community representative highlights the importance of fostering interaction between café-goers and café owners. Features such as event, discussion forums, and direct inquiries can strengthen local coffee culture and improve community engagement.
